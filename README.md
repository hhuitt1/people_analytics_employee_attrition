# People Analytics & Employee Attrition Analysis
## Project Overview

This project explores employee attrition and workforce retention trends using a fictional dataset of 5,000 employee records. SQL was used to analyze turnover patterns across departments, business units, tenure groups, work arrangements, employee engagement, satisfaction, separation types, and manager-level performance. Findings were visualized in an interactive Tableau dashboard designed to support data-driven workforce planning and retention strategies.

## Disclaimer

This project was created for portfolio and educational purposes using a fictional workforce dataset. All employee records, departments, business units, managers, and attrition events are simulated and do not represent any real organization or individuals.

### Business Questions
1.  Which departments experience the highest attrition?
2. Which business units experience the highest attrition?
3. How does engagement relate to attrition?
4. How does satisfaction relate to attrition?
5. Which tenure groups are most likely to leave?
6. What are the most common reasons employees leave?
7. Voluntary vs. involuntary separation analysis
8. How has attrition changed over time?
9. Attrition by work arrangement
10. Which managers experience the highest attrition rates?

## Key Findings

- Employee attrition increased substantially over time, rising from fewer than 10 monthly departures during the early years of the dataset to more than 50 departures per month during several months of 2025.

- Guest-facing departments experienced the highest turnover, led by Restaurant Operations (33.07%), Guest Services (32.79%), and Food & Beverage (30.10%).

- Employees in their first year reported the highest attrition rate (35.48%), indicating that turnover risk is greatest during the early stages of employment.

- Seasonal End was the most common reason for employee departures (14.02%), highlighting the impact of workforce seasonality on overall turnover.

- Onsite employees experienced significantly higher attrition (28.55%) than remote employees (19.33%), suggesting workplace flexibility may support employee retention.

- Employees who left reported lower average engagement (6.56 vs. 7.15) and satisfaction (6.63 vs. 7.30) scores than employees who remained with the organization.

- Voluntary separations accounted for 59.39% of employee departures, indicating that retention-focused initiatives may have a meaningful impact on reducing overall turnover.

- Attrition rates varied considerably across managers, with the highest-turnover teams exceeding 39%, suggesting leadership and employee experience may influence retention outcomes.
